\section{Background}

The outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic lead to an increased spread of misinformation and conspiracy theories evolving around e.g. the origin of the virus, but also about the intentions of the responsible authorities putting into effect countermeasures to prevent the further spread of the virus such as e.g. lockdowns, the mandatory use of face masks or testing, and last but not least COVID-19 vaccinations. 
 
The rise of conspiracy theories has often been accompanied by antisemitism, racism, but also hateful targeting of politicians. 

\begin{itemize}

\item racism: especially Asian community made responsible for spreading the virus
\item antisemitism: Jews identified as culprits behind the pandemic, using it as a means to obtain world dominance
\item politicians: complicit with those who benefit from pandemic; following a secret plan; restriction of civil rights

\item anti-COVID-19 measures were accompanied by protests against them 
\item freedom of speech, criticizing the government are valuable and essential components of a democratic society
\item but: often hateful tendencies and developments, both online and offline 
\item entanglement of digital and real world
\item hate does not stay in the internet, also has real effects
\item examples: ... Karl Lauterbach, Kretschmer, Demo vor Haus von Politikerin, Entführung Gesundheitsminister in Schweiz

\item spread of misinformation, conspiracy theories: social media and messengers
\item not only information, also mobilization
\item special targets: politicians, but also minority groups, e.g. persons identified as Jewish
\item journalists uncover networks, raise attention for dangerous potential of these political networks, movements
\item authorities react only slowly
\item amount of text data make it hard for reserachers, NGOs to monitor development and mobilization strategies

\end{itemize}
